# Reflection

I am very happy to conclude this project. I have been working on it quite hard non-stop for a while, and it is very rewarding to see it come to completion. I feel very proud, because I feel that I truly brought my vision of the app I wanted to make into reality. It is not perfect - there are so many features I envisioned (like having a log of past games to revist and analyze) that I didnt manage to implement because of time's sake and complexity. However, the core idea of what I wanted to achieve is there, and it works. I had Claude help me out a lot with the code aspect (though I wrote many tests myself to make sure it did well, and reviewed a lot of code in general), so the biggest challenge for me was mapping the whole app out from the concepts / reactions level. I struggled a lot to get the hang of what endpoint needs what, what each concept needs to do, and so on - I was careful to not try to one-shot the app with Claude, and took a while making sure everything it put out was good. I made many mistakes along the way, especially with figuring out how to work efficiently alongside an LLM (at some point I had it overwrite a bunch of important files based on an erroneous implementation of a concept, and didnt realize until a couple commits down the line. Cost me a big headache but thank god for version control!), but I got better as I went on. Same deal goes for concept design - I feel as if I understand it way better now, and that I am now capable of something more powerful than object oriented programming which I always thought to be the ultimate form of high-level design. 

Going back to the application itself, it was very fulfilling to see how people enjoyed the app. I learned a lot when I did the playtesting, but the biggest takeaway was on how desensitized one becomes to something when you work on it so much. I would have never imagined, for instance, the side selection prompt for tiles that can be played on either side could seem ambiguous, because I was so familiar with the app, it just made sense for me. But, of course, users have a fresh look at the app, and dont come in knowing its in-and-outs. So the biggest thing I learned it to always try to test my applications with people that have never seen them, because it can be truly shocking the things they uncover that you would have never imagined otherwise!

I do want to add one more section on my reflection, which is not related to any of the questions in the handout, but its important to me, nonetheless. This project, unfortunately, coincided with some of the worst weeks of my life... I had a medical emergency (kidney stones...) that set me back a while, and just as I recovered from that I had a very traumatic experience regarding my best friend overdosing and having to safe his life. I have been ever since at an all-time motivational (and, overall, physical and emotional) low. I add this here because, this just made working on this project exponentially harder for me. I am proud of the app I was able to make given the circumstances I was thrown into, and I want to extend my most sincere gratitude to the class staff that was supportive of me all throughout. Thank you for sticking by me, and I hope this project is enough - I know it may not be groundbreaking, but I learned a lot from it, and tried my best to make something up to te class standards.
